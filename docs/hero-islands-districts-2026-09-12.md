# Hero Islands: districts and discovery games

## What changed

The decorative Rimba Islands caption overlapped the 3D cliff because it was independently positioned over a changing camera view. The island name now lives in the introduction, and build mode reserves a separate strip above the scene for its title.

The garden editor is now an island studio with four connected districts: Home garden, Sunshine beach, Rimba grove and Discovery village. Each district has six placement spots. The last three cost 80, 150 and 240 earned Hero Coins to unlock. Earlier six-slot gardens retain their positions and purchases.

The catalogue has 13 designs, including loungers, picnic tables, ponds, arches, gazebos, fountains, windmills and a discovery monument. Designs unlock once and can be placed repeatedly. Every placed item has three tiers. Tier upgrades add a gold base and stars. Moving and rotating keep the tier; putting an item away or replacing it refunds its upgrade costs. There is no real-money purchasing.

## Play and learning

- Science Lab: predict, test and sort materials with a magnet. Darjah 3 includes copper and aluminium to challenge the misconception that all metals are magnetic.
- Time Detectives: read dated evidence and restore three timelines. Invented family and garden stories are clearly labelled. The final timeline uses real Malaysian events.
- Island Navigator: plan a route using North, East, South and West, avoid forest obstacles, then watch a delivery follow the route. Darjah 3 adds a market collection stop before the school destination.

Each game has three rounds. It pays 30 coins for its first completion at a grade and 15 for subsequent finishes. Incomplete runs do not pay. Existing Bridge, Pasar, quizzes and avatar accessories share the same wallet. Help and independent answers remain visible in the practice record.

Current playable profiles are Darjah 1 and 3. These activities practise selected skills. History and geography are explicitly enrichment, not a claim of complete KSSR coverage. The new games add three mechanics, not full subject courses. Geography uses fictional island maps, not an accurate map of Malaysia.

## Next design priorities

After observing the children play this version, the next useful expansion is learning-driven island projects: restore a habitat by identifying living things' needs; run a market with change and budgets; assemble a heritage exhibit from evidence; and deliver around a geographically accurate Malaysian map. Expand each activity's content and grade mapping before adding more currencies or reward systems.

The 24 spots and tier upgrades give longer saving goals, but are finite. Further districts should bring new learning activities and new visual building sets, not simply higher prices for the same items.
